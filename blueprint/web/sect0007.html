Paper Proposition 7.8. complete conditional implication.

Let \(r,k\in \mathbb N\), with \(r\geq 2\) and \(k\geq 4\), and let \(Q\geq 2^k\). Put

\begin{equation} \begin{gathered} p_r=\frac{2r}{2r-1}, \qquad B=C_*^{\, k^2Q^{1/(k-1/2)}},\\ \varepsilon =(2^{20}rB^{2r+8})^{-1}, \qquad C_{r,k}=(C_*r\log (2+k))^{C_*k}, \end{gathered} \end{equation}
90

where \(C_*{\gt}1\) is a sufficiently large absolute constant. Then there exist an integer \(P\leq 3^Q\) and a Ionescu–Wainger set \(\Sigma =\Sigma _{\leq k}\subset P^{-1}\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z\) with the following properties.

  1. Every rational point of denominator at most \(Q\) belongs to \(\Sigma \):

    \begin{align} \bigcup _{1\leq q\leq Q} \left\{ \frac aq\pmod1:a\in \mathbb Z\right\} \subseteq \Sigma . \end{align}

    Every element of \(\Sigma \), when written in reduced form, has denominator at most \(B\). With the choice of \(\varepsilon \) in (7.9), the corresponding major-arc parameter set is \((r,\frac12)\)-good.

  2. Let \(\nu \) be a complex measure on \(\mathbb Z_P\) with \(\| \nu \| _{\mathrm{TV}}\leq 1\), and let \(m\) be a multiplier on \(\mathbb R\) supported in \([-\varepsilon ,\varepsilon ]\). Then, for \(p_r\leq p\leq 2r\),

    \begin{align} \left\| T_{\displaystyle \sum _{\alpha \in \Sigma } \widehat\nu (\alpha )m(\, \cdot -\alpha )} \right\| _{\ell ^p(\mathbb Z)\to \ell ^p(\mathbb Z)} \leq C_{r,k} \| T_m\| _{L^p(\mathbb R)\to L^p(\mathbb R)}, \end{align}

    where the translates of \(m\) are periodized on \(\mathbb T\).

  3. Put \(\Omega =[-\varepsilon ,\varepsilon ]\times \Sigma \subset \mathbb R\times \mathbb Q/\mathbb Z.\) If \(f\in \ell ^p(\mathbb Z)\) has Fourier support in \(\pi (\Omega )\), then

    \begin{align} C_{r,k}^{-1}\| f\| _{\ell ^p(\mathbb Z)} \leq \| \mathcal S_\Omega ^{-1}f\| _{L^p(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Z})} \leq C_{r,k}\| f\| _{\ell ^p(\mathbb Z)} \end{align}

    for \(p_r\leq p\leq 2r\).

  4. Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional normed space and \(0{\lt}\delta \leq (100P)^{-1}\). If \(G:\mathbb R\times \mathbb Z_P\to V\) has real Fourier support in \([-\delta ,\delta ]\), then, for \(1\leq p\leq \infty \),

    \begin{align} \left\| \bigl(G(n,n\bmod P)\bigr)_{n\in \mathbb Z} \right\| _{\ell ^p(\mathbb Z;V)} \asymp \| G\| _{L^p(\mathbb R\times \mathbb Z_P;V)}, \end{align}

    with absolute implied constants.

Proof architecture ▼

Choose the common period, denominator envelope, and narrow width in the displayed order. Verify good coprime data and containment of all source rational points, then apply the independent multiplier and sampling interfaces. Establish the actual finite-quotient Fourier identification, both norm directions and unique interpolation. The full-domain versions are explicit external inputs, not consequences asserted from finite-input versions.

Paper Lemma 7.16. complete conditional implication.

Uniformly in \(0{\lt}\beta \leq 1\), \(J\geq 1\), and \(|\sigma _j|\leq 1\), one has, for \(1{\lt}p\leq 2\),

\begin{align} \left\| T_{\sum _{j=1}^J\sigma _j (\widehat{\widetilde M}_{2^j,\beta } -\widehat{\widetilde M}_{2^{j-1},\beta })}f \right\| _{L^p(\mathbb R)} & \ll \frac1{p-1}\| f\| _{L^p(\mathbb R)}, \\ \left\| \sup _{N\geq 2} |\widetilde M_{N,\beta }*f| \right\| _{L^p(\mathbb R)} & \ll \frac1{p-1}\| f\| _{L^p(\mathbb R)}. \end{align}

Here \(\widetilde M_{N,\beta }\) is the continuous annular measure defined in (6.2). Since (7.18) holds with the supremum over all \(N\geq 2\), it also holds when the supremum is restricted to dyadic \(N\).

Proof architecture ▼

Prove the concrete annular Fourier formulas, signed \(L^2\) multiplier estimate, kernel size and Hormander condition. Apply the general continuous theorems only after verifying their hypotheses. Establish continuity in the real scale, a measurable countable supremum, and the full \(L^p\) estimate. Routine changes of variables and power integrals belong inside this node.

Paper Proposition 7.22. complete conditional implication.

Let \(r,k,Q,P,\Sigma _{\leq k},B,\varepsilon ,p_r\), and \(C_{r,k}\) be as in Proposition 7.8. Let \(\nu \) be a complex measure on \(\mathbb Z_P\) with \(\| \nu \| _{\mathrm{TV}}\leq 1\), and let \(0{\lt}\beta \leq 1\). Define \(L_N^{\nu ,\beta }\) by

\begin{align} \widehat{L_N^{\nu ,\beta }}(\xi ) = \sum _{\alpha \in \Sigma _{\leq k}} \widehat\nu (\alpha ) \widehat{\widetilde M}_{N,\beta }(\xi -\alpha ) \eta \left(\frac{\xi -\alpha }{\varepsilon }\right). \end{align}

Then

\begin{align} \left\| \sup _{\substack {N\geq 1\\ \begin{bgroup} N\in 2^{\mathbb N} \end{bgroup}}} |L_N^{\nu ,\beta }f| \right\| _{\ell ^{p_r}(\mathbb Z)} \ll rC_{r,k}^2 \log \bigl(e+Q+r\log B\bigr) \| f\| _{\ell ^{p_r}(\mathbb Z)}. \end{align}

The implied constant is independent of \(\nu \) and \(\beta \).

such that

Proof architecture ▼

Identify the actual low operators with sampled continuous annular multipliers, controlling the real-\(Q\) construction and the two low maxima jointly. Bound prime and ordinary-square high parts using their different approximation inputs. Keep the quantitative parameters before optimizing them. The internally proved finite signed maximal inequality is used instead of leaving it as an assumption.

Paper Theorem 7.2. complete conditional implication.

There are absolute constants \(C,C_0\geq 1\) such that the following holds. Choose \(A_N\) from \(A_{N,\Lambda }\), \(A_{N,\mathbb N^2}\), and \(A_{N,\mathbb P^2}\), and put \(A^*f=\sup _{N\geq 2}|A_Nf|\). Then, for every finitely supported \(f:\mathbb Z\to \mathbb C\) and every \(\lambda {\gt}0\),

\begin{align} \bigl|\{ x\in \mathbb Z:A^*f(x){\gt}\lambda \} \bigr| \ll \sum _{\substack {x\in \mathbb Z\\ \begin{bgroup} |f(x)|{\gt}\lambda /C_0 \end{bgroup}}} \Psi _C\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda }\right). \end{align}

Consequently,

\begin{align} \bigl|\{ x\in \mathbb Z:A^*f(x){\gt}\lambda \} \bigr| \ll \sum _{x\in \mathbb Z} \Psi _C\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda }\right). \end{align}

In particular, for every finite \(F\subset \mathbb Z\) and \(t\geq 1\),

\begin{align} \bigl|\{ x\in \mathbb Z:A^*\mathbf1_F(x){\gt}t^{-1}\} \bigr| \ll |F|\Psi _C(t). \end{align}
Proof architecture ▼

Optimize the quantitative low-high comparison to obtain the restricted distribution estimate. Assemble the von Mangoldt, ordinary-square and prime-square families using the proved full/annular and prime-power comparisons. Extend the restricted estimate to the convex gauge by level decomposition; remove the bounded part to obtain the truncated modular form.

Paper Corollary 7.6. complete conditional implication.

For \(1{\lt}p\leq 2\) and \(f\in \ell ^p(\mathbb Z)\),

\begin{align} \| A^*f\| _{\ell ^p} \leq \exp \left( \frac{C}{p-1}\log \frac{e}{p-1} \right)\| f\| _{\ell ^p}, \end{align}

after enlarging the absolute constant \(C\).

Proof architecture ▼

Integrate the distribution estimate against the layer-cake weight and estimate the gauge moment with the displayed dependence on \(p-1\). Extend from finite support using positive finite averages, norm convergence and monotone maxima. The resulting constant is retained for the ergodic theorem.
