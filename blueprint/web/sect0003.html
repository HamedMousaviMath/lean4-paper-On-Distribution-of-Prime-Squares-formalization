Definition 2.1 Quadratic sums and progression coefficients
✓

Paper Actual quadratic exponential sums. definition formalized.

For a Dirichlet character \(\chi \) modulo \(q\), put

\begin{align} \mathfrak s_\chi (a,q) =\frac1{\phi (q)}\sum _{r\in \mathbb A_q} \chi (r)e\left(-\frac{ar^2}{q}\right). \end{align}

For the principal character we write

\begin{align} \mathfrak s(a,q) =\frac1{\phi (q)}\sum _{r\in \mathbb A_q} e\left(-\frac{ar^2}{q}\right). \end{align}

Paper Actual normalized progression sum Upsilon. definition formalized.

Let \(g=(q,y)\) and \((b,g)=1\). Define

\begin{align} \Upsilon (a,q,b,y)=\frac{\phi (y)}{\phi ([y,q])} \sum _{\substack {r\in \mathbb A_q\\ \begin{bgroup} r\equiv b\pmod g \end{bgroup}}} e\left(-\frac{ar}{q}\right). \end{align}
Proof architecture ▼

Use actual finite sums over units, with characters extended by zero. Preserve the normalization by \(\phi (q)\) and the Fourier sign. All later local calculations refer to these definitions, rather than an abstract family of coefficients.

Paper Lemma 3.1. proved under source hypotheses.

Let \(2^a\parallel q\). Then

\begin{align} \sum _{r\in \mathbb A_q}c_q(n-r^2) =|\mu (q/2^a)| T_a(n) \prod _{\substack {p\mid q\\ \begin{bgroup} p{\gt}2 \end{bgroup}}} \begin{cases} p+1,& \left(\frac np\right)=1,\\ 1-p,& \left(\frac np\right)\ne 1, \end{cases}\end{align}

where

\begin{align} T_a(n)= \begin{cases} 1,& a=0,\\ (-1)^{n+1},& a=1,\\ 0,& a=2,3\text{ and }n\not\equiv 1\pmod{2^{a-1}},\\ \phi (2^a)c_{2^a}(n-1)=\pm \phi (2^a)^2, & a=2,3\text{ and }n\equiv 1\pmod{2^{a-1}},\\ 0,& a\geq 4. \end{cases}\end{align}

Consequently,

\begin{align} \left|\sum _{r\in \mathbb A_q}c_q(n-r^2)\right| \ll |\mu (q/2^a)|\prod _{p\mid q}(p+1). \end{align}

Here \((\frac np)\) is the Legendre symbol, with value zero when \(p\mid n\).

Paper Lemma 3.11. proved under source hypotheses.

Let \(g\mid q\) and \((b,g)=1\). Then

\begin{align} \sum _{\substack {r\in \mathbb A_q\\ \begin{bgroup} r\equiv b\pmod g \end{bgroup}}}c_q(n-r) = \begin{cases} 0,& (g,q/g){\gt}1,\\ \mu (q/g)c_{q/g}(n)c_g(n-b),& (g,q/g)=1. \end{cases}\end{align}
Proof architecture ▼

Expand the Ramanujan sum and group by residue classes. Reduce to prime powers using the Chinese remainder theorem, retaining the shared-prime vanishing case. The proof includes the positive-modulus boundary and the actual local root counts; routine finite-sum rearrangements stay within this node.

Paper Lemma 3.12. proved under source hypotheses.

Let \(g=(q,y)\) and \((b,g)=1\). Then

\begin{align} & \frac{\phi (q)\phi ([y,q])}{\phi (y)} \sum _{a\in \mathbb A_q}\Upsilon (a,q,b,y) \mathfrak s(a,q)e\left(\frac{ax}{q}\right)\notag \\ & \qquad =\mu (q/g)\mathbf1_{(g,q/g)=1} \sum _{u\in \mathbb A_q}c_{q/g}(x-u^2)c_g(x-b-u^2). \end{align}

In particular, the right-hand side vanishes unless \(q/g\) is square-free; if \((g,q/g)=1\), it factors over the prime powers dividing \(q\).

Paper Lemma 3.14. proved under source hypotheses.

Let \(Q{\gt}2\), let \(k\geq 1\) be an integer, and let \(x,b\in \mathbb Z\) satisfy \((b,k)=1\). Then

\begin{align} & \sum _{P^+(q){\lt}Q}\sum _{a\in \mathbb A_q} \Upsilon (a,q,b,k)\mathfrak s(a,q)e(ax/q)\notag \\ & =\mathcal T_2 \prod _{\substack {2{\lt}p{\lt}Q\\ \begin{bgroup} p\mid k \\ \end{bgroup}\begin{bgroup} (\frac{x-b}{p})=1 \end{bgroup}}} \left(1+\frac{p+1}{p-1}\right) \prod _{\substack {2{\lt}p{\lt}Q\\ \begin{bgroup} p\mid k \\ \end{bgroup}\begin{bgroup} (\frac{x-b}{p})\ne 1 \end{bgroup}}}0 \prod _{\substack {2{\lt}p{\lt}Q\\ \begin{bgroup} p\nmid k \\ \end{bgroup}\begin{bgroup} (\frac{x}{p})=1 \end{bgroup}}} \left(1-\frac{p+1}{(p-1)^2}\right) \prod _{\substack {2{\lt}p{\lt}Q\\ \begin{bgroup} p\nmid k \\ \end{bgroup}\begin{bgroup} (\frac{x}{p})\ne 1 \end{bgroup}}} \left(1+\frac1{p-1}\right), \end{align}

where, writing \(h=v_2(k)\),

\begin{align} \mathcal T_2= \begin{cases} 1+(-1)^x,& h=0,\\ 2^{\min \{ h,3\} } \mathbf1_{\{ x-b\equiv 1\; (\mathrm{mod}\; 2^{\min \{ h,3\} })\} }, & h\geq 1. \end{cases}\end{align}

Thus the product is zero if some odd prime \(p{\lt}Q\) dividing \(k\) satisfies \(\bigl(\frac{x-b}{p}\bigr)\ne 1\).

Paper Corollary 3.16. proved under source hypotheses.

Let \(Q{\gt}2\), let \(k\geq 1\) be an integer, and let \(\ell ,n,b\in \mathbb Z\) satisfy \((b,k)=1\). Then

\begin{align} & \sum _{P^+(q){\lt}Q}\sum _{a\in \mathbb A_q} \Upsilon (a\ell ,q,b,k)\mathfrak s(a,q)e(an/q)\notag \\ & \qquad = \mathcal T_2(\ell ,k,n;b) \prod _{2{\lt}p{\lt}Q} \frac{p\phi ((p,k))}{(p-1)^2} \left|\left\{ (u,v)\in \mathbb A_p^2: \begin{array}{l} u^2+\ell v\equiv n\pmod p,\\ v\equiv b\pmod{(p,k)} \end{array}\right\} \right|, \end{align}

where

\begin{align} \mathcal T_2(\ell ,k,n;b) := 1+\sum _{\nu =1}^{3} \frac{\phi (k)}{\phi (2^\nu )\phi ([k,2^\nu ])} \sum _{\substack {u,v\in \mathbb A_{2^\nu }\\ \begin{bgroup} v\equiv b\; (\mathrm{mod}\, (2^\nu ,k)) \end{bgroup}}} c_{2^\nu }(n-u^2-\ell v). \end{align}
Proof architecture ▼

Factor the normalized correlation and sum the local series. Treat odd primes dividing the progression modulus separately from those not dividing it. At \(2\), retain the truncated valuation \(\min (v_2(k),3)\) and the corresponding congruence indicator. The obstruction is only at primes in the displayed cutoff range. The multiplier version uses the actual residue counts.

For \(Q\geq 3\) and every integer \(n\),

\begin{align} \sum _{P^+(q){\lt}Q}\frac1{q\phi (q)} \left|\sum _{r\in \mathbb A_q}c_q(n-r^2)\right| \ll \log Q. \end{align}

Paper Lemma 3.21. complete conditional implication.

If \((a,q)=1\), then

\begin{align} |\mathfrak s(a,q)| \leq q^{-1/2} \exp \left((2+o(1))\frac{(\log q)^{1/2}}{\log \log q}\right). \end{align}

In particular, the right-hand side may be replaced by \(q^{-1/2}\exp (3(\log q)^{1/2}/\log \log q)\) for all sufficiently large \(q\).

Paper Lemma 3.24. proved under source hypotheses.

Let \(\chi \) be a real Dirichlet character modulo \(q\), and let \((a,q)=1\). Then, for every \(\varepsilon {\gt}0\),

\begin{align} |\mathfrak s_\chi (a,q)|\ll _\varepsilon q^{-1/2+\varepsilon }. \end{align}

Equivalently, \( |\mathfrak s_\chi (a,q)|\ll q^{-1/2+o(1)}\).

Proof architecture ▼

Use prime-power evaluations and multiplicativity for the character-twisted estimate. For the sharp principal bound, optimize the product of local losses and use the two explicit prime asymptotic limits. Keep the quantified eventual epsilon bound: the coefficient \(2+o(1)\) is not a fixed uniform value of \(2\). Only the principal asymptotic clause uses the external PNT input.

Paper Lemma 3.27. proved under source hypotheses.

Let \(x\in \mathbb Z\). Let \(\chi _0\) be a primitive real character of conductor \(q_0\), and for each multiple \(q\) of \(q_0\) let \(\chi _q\) denote the character modulo \(q\) induced by \(\chi _0\). For \(0\leq j\leq 5\), let \(\chi _{2^jq_0}\) have the same meaning, and set

\[ \sigma _0:= \max _{0\leq j\leq 5} \sup _{a\in \mathbb A_{2^jq_0}} |\mathfrak s_{\chi _{2^jq_0}}(a,2^jq_0)|, \qquad \rho (d):=\prod _{p\mid d}(1-p^{-1/2})^{-1}. \]

There exists \(\mathcal B_0\geq 1\), depending only on \(q_0\), such that, for every \(q=q_0d\),

\begin{align} \left| \sum _{a\in \mathbb A_q} \mathfrak s_{\chi _q}(a,q)e(ax/q) \right| & \ll \mathcal B_0\prod _{p\mid d}\frac{p+1}{p-1},\notag \\ |\mathfrak s_{\chi _q}(a,q)| & \ll \sigma _0d^{-1/2}\rho (d), \qquad \textup{ for } a\in \mathbb {A}_q\\ \sigma _0\mathcal B_0^{1/2}& \ll q_0^{-1/25}. \end{align}

In particular,

\[ (1+\log q_0)\sigma _0\mathcal B_0^{1/2}\ll 1. \]

Moreover, if \(a\mapsto \mathfrak s_{\chi _q}(a,q)\) is not identically zero, then

\begin{align} d=2^\nu d_1,\qquad 0\leq \nu \leq 5,\qquad \mu ^2(d_1)=1,\qquad (d_1,2q_0)=1. \end{align}
Proof architecture ▼

Write each induced character through its primitive conductor. The support and coefficient bounds must retain this conductor before summing over the remaining factor of the denominator. Derive the three displayed estimates and logarithmic consequence together; the hypothesis is strengthened to real base characters, so it covers the exceptional character selected later.
