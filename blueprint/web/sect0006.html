Definition 5.1 Sparse families and admissible stopping maps
✓
#

  • PrimeSquares.IntegerSparseCollection
  • PrimeSquares.SourceLinearAdmissible

Paper Definition 2.8. definition formalized.

A collection \(\mathcal S\) of intervals is sparse if, for every \(I\in \mathcal S\), there is \(E_I\subset I\) such that \(|E_I|\geq |I|/4\) and the sets \(E_I\), \(I\in \mathcal S\), are pairwise disjoint.

Paper Definition 2.9. definition formalized.

Let \(f=\mathbf1_F\) be supported in \(3I_0\). A map \(\tau :I_0\longrightarrow \{ 2^j:1\leq 2^j\leq |I_0|\} \) is admissible if, for every \(x\in I_0\),

\begin{align} \sup _{M\geq \tau (x)}\frac1M \sum _{|y-x|\leq CM}f(y) \leq C_{\rm adm}\frac{|F|}{|I_0|}. \end{align}

where \(C{\gt}1\) is a fixed radius constant and \(C_{\rm adm}{\gt}1\) is a sufficiently large fixed constant depending on \(C\). Changing the fixed radius changes only the required admissibility constant. One may also replace the symmetric local average by the maximum of the left and right one-sided local averages; these quantities are comparable with a factor of at most two.

Proof architecture ▼

Keep the sparsity parameter explicit: the preliminary convention is \(1/4\), whereas the introductory sparse theorem uses \(1/10\). Admissibility is a symmetric spatial density condition; a single one-sided bound does not suffice. The radius constant and the sufficiently large admissibility coefficient are distinct parameters.

Paper Lemma 5.1. proved under source hypotheses.

Fix constants \(C\geq 1\) and \(c{\gt}2C+1\). Let \((B_M)\) be a family of positive convolution operators satisfying

\begin{align} |B_M f(x)| \ll M^{-v}\| f\mathbf1_{[x-CM,x+CM]}\| _1, \qquad 0\leq v{\lt}1. \end{align}

Let \(s\geq 1-v\), \(r{\gt}0\), and \(\gamma \geq \beta \geq 0\), and suppose that, whenever \(M\) is dyadic and \(2\leq J\leq M^{(1-v)/s}/10\),

\begin{align} B_M=Lo_{J,M}+Hi_{J,M}+Er_{J,M}. \end{align}

Let \(I_0\) be an interval, and let \(f=\mathbf1_F\) and \(g=\mathbf1_G\), where \(F\subset cI_0\) and \(G\subset I_0\). Suppose that

\[ \tau :I_0\longrightarrow \{ 2^k:2^k\leq |I_0|\} \]

satisfies

\begin{align} \sup _{M\geq \tau (x)}M^{-1} \sum _{|y-x|\leq C_0M}f(y) \ll \langle f\rangle _{cI_0,1}, \qquad x\in I_0. \end{align}

Write \(B_\tau f(x)=B_{\tau (x)}f(x)\). Assume that, for every \(J\geq 2\) and every \(x\in I_0\) such that \(\tau (x)\geq (10J)^{s/(1-v)}\),

\begin{align} |Lo_{J,\tau (x)}f(x)| & \ll J^s(\log J)^\gamma \sup _{M\geq \tau (x)}M^{-1} \sum _{|y-x|\leq CM}f(y), \end{align}

and that, for every \(h\in \ell ^2(\mathbb Z)\),

\begin{align} \left\| \sup _{\substack {M\geq (10J)^{s/(1-v)}\\ \begin{bgroup} M\ {\rm dyadic} \end{bgroup}}} |Hi_{J,M}h| \right\| _2 & \ll J^{-r}(\log J)^\beta \| h\| _2. \end{align}

Finally, suppose that, for some fixed \(A{\gt}1/2\), uniformly in dyadic \(M\),

\begin{align} \| \widehat{Er}_{J,M}\| _{L^\infty (\mathbb T)} \ll _A J^{-r}(\log J)^\beta (1+\log M)^{-A}. \end{align}

Then

\begin{align} |I_0|^{-1}|\langle B_\tau f,g\rangle | \ll _c \left(\frac{|F||G|}{|I_0|^2}\right)^{ 1-\frac{s}{2(s+r)}} \left(\operatorname {Log}\frac{|F||G|}{|I_0|^2}\right)^{ \gamma +\frac{s(\beta -\gamma )}{s+r}}. \end{align}

Paper Lemma 5.10. proved under source hypotheses.

Suppose that every admissible \(\tau \) satisfies

\begin{align} |\langle T_\tau \mathbf1_F,\mathbf1_G\rangle | \ll |I_0|\, \Psi \left(\frac{|F|}{|I_0|}, \frac{|G|}{|I_0|}\right) \end{align}

for \(F\subset cI_0\) and \(G\subset I_0\), where \((T_N)_{N\in 2^{\mathbb N}}\) is a family of positive convolution operators whose kernel is supported in \([-CN,CN]\) for some fixed \(C{\gt}0\). Then there is a sparse family \(\mathcal S\) such that

\begin{align} \langle \sup _{N\in 2^{\mathbb N}}|T_N\mathbf1_F|,\mathbf1_G\rangle \ll \sum _{I\in \mathcal S}|I|\, \Psi \left(\frac{|F\cap cI|}{|I|}, \frac{|G\cap I|}{|I|}\right). \end{align}
Proof architecture ▼

First establish the local estimate from the displayed Fourier and admissibility hypotheses. Stop on intervals where local densities exceed the chosen threshold, and construct disjoint major subsets with the required sparsity. Iterate a finite dyadic tree and pass to the source supremum through finite tests. Keep the actual stopping-map admissibility and the sufficiently large threshold in the recursion.

Paper Lemma 5.17. complete conditional implication.

Let \(f=\mathbf1_F\) and \(g=\mathbf1_G\), where \(F\subset cI_0\) and \(G\subset I_0\), and put \(\delta _0=\frac{|F||G|}{|I_0|^2}.\) Let \(\tau :I_0\to \{ 2^k:(2^k)^2\leq |I_0|\} \) be admissible at the spatial scale \(\tau (x)^2\), in the sense that

\begin{align} \sup _{M\geq \tau (x)}M^{-2} \sum _{|y-x|\leq CM^2}f(y) \ll \langle f\rangle _{cI_0,1}. \end{align}

Under the hypotheses of Proposition 4.19,

\begin{align} |I_0|^{-1} \big|\langle \widetilde A_{\tau ,\Lambda }\mathbf1_F, \mathbf1_G\rangle \big| \ll \delta _0^{2/3} \Phi _{4\sqrt3/9+o(1)}(\delta _0). \end{align}
Proof architecture ▼

Insert the selected prime decomposition and the independent ordinary-square stopping estimates into the abstract local theorem. Convert square-scale spatial radii to the source stopping convention and retain dilation constants. The general fixed-scale ordinary-square decomposition alone does not supply this stronger stopping input.

Paper Theorem 1.3. complete conditional implication.

Let \(F,G\subset \mathbb Z\) be finite. There is a sparse collection of intervals \(\mathcal S\) such that

\begin{align} \bigl|\langle A_\Lambda ^*\mathbf1_F,\mathbf1_G\rangle \bigr| & \ll \sum _{I\in \mathcal S}|I| \left(\frac{|F\cap I||G\cap I|}{|I|^2}\right)^{2/3} \Phi _{4\sqrt3/9+o(1)} \left(\frac{|F\cap I||G\cap I|}{|I|^2}\right). \end{align}

Moreover, if \((1/p,1/q)\) lies in the interior of the triangle with vertices \((2/3,2/3),\) \((1,0)\), and \((0,1)\), then there is a sparse collection \(\mathcal S_{p,q}\) such that

\begin{align} \bigl|\langle A_\Lambda ^*\mathbf1_F,\mathbf1_G\rangle \bigr| & \ll _{p,q} \sum _{I\in \mathcal S_{p,q}}|I| \left(\frac{|F\cap I|}{|I|}\right)^{1/p} \left(\frac{|G\cap I|}{|I|}\right)^{1/q}. \end{align}
Proof architecture ▼

Linearize finite maxima, apply the local endpoint recursively, and pass to the actual maximal pairing. Interpolate the endpoint form with complementary-exponent information from the independent prime maximal theorem to reach the open triangle. Use the stated \(1/10\) sparsity convention in both conclusions.
