Paper Lemma 8.8. complete conditional implication.

Let \(p\) be an odd prime and let

\[ \Lambda _p=\{ x^2\pmod p:x\not\equiv 0\pmod p\} . \]

For every fixed \(k\geq 1\) and distinct \(0,h_1,\ldots ,h_{k-1}\pmod p\),

\[ N_k(\mathbf h,\Lambda _p)=\frac{p}{2^k}+O_k(p^{1/2}) =\left(\frac{p-1}{2p}\right)^kp\big(1+O_k(p^{-1/2})\big). \]

Consequently (8.7) holds, for example with \(c=1/3\).

Proof architecture ▼

Expand products of the quadratic-residue indicators. Separate zero factors and repeated shifts, then use the independent split quadratic Weil bound for the genuinely nonconstant terms. Control the resulting error uniformly in the allowed tuples and prove the needed subpower density bound.

Paper Theorem 8.1. complete conditional implication.

Let \((n_k)\) be an increasing sequence of natural numbers. Suppose that, for every \(q{\gt}1\), there is a set \(\Lambda _q\subset \{ n_k\bmod q:k\geq 1\} \) which is multiplicative under the Chinese remainder theorem: if \((q,r)=1\), then \(\Lambda _{qr}\) corresponds to \(\Lambda _q\times \Lambda _r\). Suppose also that there are two sequences \((u_j)\) and \((q_j)\) of odd integers whose combined collection is pairwise coprime and such that

\begin{align} & \mathbb P_{q_j}(\Lambda _{q_j})\longrightarrow 0, \qquad \inf _j\mathbb P_{u_j}(\Lambda _{u_j}){\gt}0, \\ & \gamma -\liminf _{j\to \infty } \mathbb P_{q_j}(\Lambda _{q_j}^{\gamma })=o(\gamma ) \quad (\gamma \downarrow 0), \end{align}

and that there is an \(\alpha {\gt}0\) such that, for every sufficiently small \(\gamma {\gt}0\),

\begin{align} \liminf _{j\to \infty }\frac1{|\Lambda _{q_j}|^2} \big|\{ (u,v)\in \Lambda _{q_j}^2: \operatorname {dist}_{q_j}(u-v,\Lambda _{q_j}){\gt} \gamma s_{q_j}\} \big|{\gt}5\alpha . \end{align}

Finally, suppose that some \(\beta {\gt}0\) has the following property. If \(Q\) is any product of distinct terms from the two sequences \((u_j)\) and \((q_j)\), then for every \(a\in \Lambda _Q\),

\begin{align} \liminf _{N\to \infty }\frac1N \big|\{ 1\leq k\leq N:n_k\equiv a\pmod Q\} \big| {\gt}\frac{\beta }{|\Lambda _Q|}. \end{align}

Then \((n_k)\) is persistently universally \(L^1\)-bad.

Proof architecture ▼

Construct finite cyclic blocks with the required arithmetic, local moments and growth conditions. Push the fixed-progression prime frequencies through the exact prime-square residue fibres. Apply the original general LaVictoire criterion and the Sawyer and Conze inputs to obtain divergence along every infinite cutoff set. The optional full Poisson-spacing theorem is not required for this route.
