# General finite signed-sum maximal boundary

Checkpoint 904 records `RawFiniteSignedMaximalInput C`. This is a general finite-sequence consequence of Rademacher-Menshov and scalar Khintchine, uniform for 1<=p<=2. It ranges over arbitrary complex sequences u_j(x) on integer counting measure, arbitrary finite length J and arbitrary extended norm bound B. If every complex signed sum with |sigma_j|<=1 has actual Lp membership and norm at most B, the maximum of all partial sums through J has actual Lp membership and norm at most C log(2+J) B. No Fourier symbol, arithmetic data, cutoff, annular kernel, IW construction or current-paper maximum occurs in its fields.

Its general existence is not proved in Lean and it is not a Lean axiom. The boundary includes the deterministic dyadic decomposition, scalar Khintchine, Fubini and the Lp triangle inequality. A primary source for the decomposition is Mirek, Stein and Zorin-Kranich, *A bootstrapping approach to jump inequalities and their applications*, Lemma 2.5, equation (2.7), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.09048 . Scalar Khintchine and its function-valued Fubini consequence are also recorded in Tao's *Lecture notes 4 for 247A*, Corollary 5.8, https://www.math.ucla.edu/~tao/247a.1.06f/notes4.pdf .

For clarity, the quantitative consequence is obtained by padding to a power of two and grouping increments in dyadic intervals at each level. Choosing one random sign per disjoint interval produces a permitted signed sum of the original u_j. Khintchine's lower bound at exponent 1, followed by monotonicity of probability Lp norms, bounds the square function uniformly for 1<=p<=2. The deterministic inequality and the Lp triangle inequality then give the number of levels, which is bounded by a universal multiple of log(2+J). This general argument remains in the explicit unformalized input, rather than being counted as a Lean proof.

The paper-specific signed sums are proved for the actual IW annular operators, with exact identities and K*r*C_(r,k) bounds. Their instantiation of this general theorem, and adding the checked initial term, are separate checked deductions. The entire low maximum, its narrow large-scale sampling argument, and the restricted maximal estimate are never external inputs.
