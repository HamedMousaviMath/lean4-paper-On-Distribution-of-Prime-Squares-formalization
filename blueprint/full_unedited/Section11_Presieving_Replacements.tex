% 1. Replace the passage beginning "Let \(2\leq R<z\)" and ending
% with the displayed condition \log D_0/\log R\longrightarrow\infty by:

Assume \(X\geq0\) and \(g(1)=1\).
Fix constants \(C,A>0\), put
\[
 R=(\log N)^C,\qquad
 D_0=\exp((\log\log N)^3),\qquad D_1=D/D_0,
\]
and work with sufficiently large \(N\). Assume
\[
 R+1\leq z\leq D_1\leq N^A,\qquad D_0<D.
\]
Write
\[
 \mathfrak P_0=\{p\in\mathfrak P:p\leq R\},\qquad
 \mathfrak P_1=\{p\in\mathfrak P:R<p<z\}.
\]
Suppose that there are fixed constants
\(\kappa_0,\kappa>0\) and \(K_0\geq2\), uniform in the sequence and
its density function, such that for every \(2\leq w<y\leq z\),
\begin{align}\label{e:tworangesievedimension}
 \prod_{\substack{w\leq p<y\\p\in\mathfrak P_0}}
 (1-g(p))^{-1}
 &\leq
 \left(1+\frac{K_0}{\log w}\right)
 \left(\frac{\log y}{\log w}\right)^{\kappa_0},\notag\\
 \prod_{\substack{w\leq p<y\\p\in\mathfrak P_1}}
 (1-g(p))^{-1}
 &\leq
 \left(1+\frac{K_0}{\log w}\right)
 \left(\frac{\log y}{\log w}\right)^\kappa.
\end{align}
All asymptotic assertions below are uniform when these constants are fixed.

Apply the general Rosser--Iwaniec coefficient theorem to
\(\mathfrak P_0\), level \(D_0\), and sifting limit \(R+1\).
Its coefficient estimates and the decay of the sieve functions and error
majorant give constants \(c,C'>0\), depending only on the fixed sieve
parameters, such that
\[
 \left|\sum_e\lambda_{0,e}^{\pm}g(e)-V_0(R)\right|
 \leq C'V_0(R)e^{-c s_0}
\]
for sufficiently large \(N\). Since
\[
 s_0=\frac{(\log\log N)^3}{\log((\log N)^C+1)}
 \gg_C(\log\log N)^2,
\]
the relative preliminary error is smaller than every fixed negative
power of \(\log N\). The condition \(z\leq D_1\) places the outer
sieve in its stated range \(s\geq1\), and \(z\leq N^A\) permits this
preliminary error to absorb the logarithmic losses from the outer
coefficient sum. The signed coefficient construction in the remainder
of the proof is unchanged.

% the following passage. A coarse implied-constant estimate by itself
% does not supply the refined dimension hypothesis above.

We use the uniform interval forms of Mertens' theorem: for a suitable
constant \(K\) and every \(2\leq w<y\),
\begin{align*}
 \prod_{\substack{w\leq p<y\\p\equiv3\; (\mathrm{mod}\;4)}}
 \left(1-\frac1{p-1}\right)^{-1}
 &\leq
 \left(1+\frac K{\log w}\right)
 \left(\frac{\log y}{\log w}\right)^{1/2},\\
 \prod_{\substack{w\leq p<y\\p>2}}
 \left(1-\frac1{p-1}\right)^{-1}
 &\leq
 \left(1+\frac K{\log w}\right)
 \frac{\log y}{\log w}.
\end{align*}
Restricting these products to the relevant outer prime sets only
decreases them. For the preliminary prime sets, the local bounds
\(g(p)\leq2/(p-3)\) for \(p\geq7\), together with the bound
\(g(5)\leq1/2\) when that prime occurs, give the refined dimension
bound with \(\kappa_0=2\) and a uniform constant. Thus only the outer
dimensions \(1/2\) and \(1\) determine the final sieve functions.
